# RAID — mcp-lineage Workstream

<!-- meta-id: 0b094282dc86ccb3 -->

**Scope:** One interaction — an LLM/agent calls an MCP tool that touches a data resource. Client, server and data resource only. **MCP gateways, proxies and federation are out of scope** (see `README.md` § Scope); entries touching them are recorded and deferred, not actioned. Reference implementation + demo supporting [OpenLineage #4484](https://github.com/OpenLineage/OpenLineage/issues/4484) and [MCP #2638](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/issues/2638). Epic: [DIO11y-lab-v2#2](https://github.com/BearingNode/DIO11y-lab-v2/issues/2).

## How to Use This File

This is a **register**, not a log. Entries are **live records that are updated
in place** as the position changes — a risk's likelihood moves, a mitigation
lands, an issue closes, an assumption gets tested.

**Reading it:** `grep -n` for the ID you need. Do not read end to end unless
you are auditing it.

**Publication:** this is the published copy of the register, curated for a
public audience — trimmed of entries with no bearing on the workstream's
findings, never of corrections or invalidated assumptions. See the lab's
`CURATION.md` for the general policy this curation follows.

### The four registers

This register applies BearingNode's **Anatomy of Uncertainty** framework. Each
quadrant demands a different response, so filing something in the wrong register
means responding to it the wrong way:

| Quadrant | Register | What it demands |
|----------|----------|-----------------|
| Known Knowns | **Decisions** | execution |
| Known Knowns | **Issues** | issue management, corrective action |
| Known Unknowns | **Risks** | mitigation |
| Unknown Knowns | **Assumptions** | surfacing — you cannot mitigate an assumption you do not know you are making |
| Unknown Unknowns | reversibility built into decisions | resilience, not planning |

The framework is set out in a three-part series on the BearingNode blog:

1. **[Anatomy of Uncertainty: Why 95% of GenAI Pilots Fail](https://www.bearingnode.com/post/anatomy-of-uncertainty-why-data-ai-projects-need-a-new-navigation-map)**
   — the four quadrants, and why data and AI projects need a different
   navigation map.
2. **[The Eight Uncertainty Scenarios in Data & AI Projects](https://www.bearingnode.com/post/the-eight-scenarios-mapping-positive-and-negative-uncertainty-in-data-ai-projects)**
   — each quadrant splits into a **positive and a negative** scenario, giving
   eight situations that demand different governance approaches; traditional
   PMO works for four of them and actively harms the other four.
3. **[Your PMO is Killing Your AI: The Governance Mismatch](https://www.bearingnode.com/post/anatomy-of-uncertainty-part-3-why-ai-projects-need-different-governance)**
   — why AI projects need different governance from delivery projects.

**Uncertainty here is two-sided, and the registers carry both.** Per Part 2,
each quadrant splits into a positive and a negative scenario. There is
deliberately **no separate Opportunities register** (I19) — where an entry has
upside as well as downside, say so **in the entry**. R06 is the pattern: the
same fact that makes per-server instrumentation an adoption risk makes gateways
a concentration opportunity, and both belong in one place because they are one
fact. Look for the upside when writing an entry; do not file it separately.

### Rules

- **IDs are stable and never reused.** They are cited from commit messages,
  code comments and GitHub issues. Renumbering breaks them.
- **Update entries in place.** Never silently rewrite.
- **A decision is not complete until its implications are logged.** Where a
  decision *creates* a risk or issue, open it and tag it `Created by Dnn`, and
  list it in that decision's **Implications** column. A decision whose costs
  live only in its Rationale reads as free, and the next reader cannot find
  what it cost.
- **Record wrong assumptions as wrong — never delete them.** The correction is
  the most valuable content here (see A02, A04, A06).
- **Negative decisions are decisions** (D02, D16). Deliberately accepting
  something is a decision too. An accepted risk stays **Open — accepted**
  rather than being closed to tidy the register.
- **Dates are absolute, `YYYY-MM-DD`.**
- Anything needing tracking beyond this file becomes a **repo issue**.

| R01 | MCP may have no standard mechanism for a client to pass caller/agent identity through to a server (session metadata, `_meta`, or equivalent) — if so, D01's split-emission design can't populate actor identity without a bespoke convention. | D01. | High. Verified against the primary spec text: the draft reserves `io.modelcontextprotocol/clientInfo`, which is client software name and version only. MCP carries no principal, agent, or on-behalf-of identity, and no authority model, in any version of the spec. | High — undermines the actor-identity half of the emitted event, and is itself a second concrete MCP-spec gap worth raising. | The carrier for identity exists (`_meta`, with a documented extension path); the content doesn't. | R02 | If actor/identity facets carry raw human-identifying data (name, email, user ID) rather than a pseudonymous reference, PII propagates into OpenLineage stores (e.g. Marquez) never designed with retention limits, access controls, or right-to-erasure. | R03 | Parse-derived lineage is silently incomplete. Tables reached through views, triggers, cascading deletes, partition routing, row-level security, or reads inside functions and procedures don't appear in the emitted event — and nothing indicates they're missing. The graph looks complete and isn't. | Created by D08. | High — the normal case, not the edge case. | R04 | A reviewer reads D08 as doing exactly what D02 and A01 rejected — deriving dataset identity by parsing SQL text. If that lands, it discredits the "correlate, never derive" principle by making it look situational. | Created by D08. | Medium — the objection is obvious to anyone reading this register, which is intended to be published. | High — attacks the strongest argument at its foundation. "We did it inside the server" sounds like special pleading unless the distinction is drawn before being challenged. | State the distinction in the RFC text, not just here — D02 is about **who declares** (the entity that executed the statement), not **how it knows**. An external proxy's parse is a guess about someone else's action; the server's parse is the acting entity declaring its own. Pair with R03's honesty about incompleteness, which is what makes the position credible rather than convenient. | Open. |
| R06 | D01 puts dataset-identity emission in the server, because only the server executes the query. Taken alone that implies per-server instrumentation — every MCP server in an estate individually forked or patched before any lineage exists. Or does an MCP gateway carry part of it instead? | D01, D02. | Certain — this is a property of the design, not a possibility. What's uncertain is which way it cuts. | Downside — an RFC whose adoption path is "fork every server you run" won't be adopted, however correct it is; an adoption risk to the whole proposal, not an implementation detail. Upside — gateways are a concentration point (few implementations, many servers behind them) and already own the identity/auth context D01 assigns to the caller. | Verified directly against [`bn-mcp-context-forge`](https://github.com/BearingNode/bn-mcp-context-forge) — BearingNode's public fork of [`IBM/mcp-context-forge`](https://github.com/IBM/mcp-context-forge) — it has a plugin framework (`tool_pre_invoke`/`tool_post_invoke`, `mcpgateway/plugins/framework/hooks/tools.py`) and already carries OTel tracing (`mcpgateway/observability.py`, `create_span`) but no lineage equivalent. A plugin can see the caller (`user`, `tenant_id` via `GlobalContext`) but cannot read or inject MCP `_meta` through the supported extension point, so it can't hand the server a parent run to link to — and even with that fixed, a plugin still can't emit dataset identity, since most of `postgres-mcp`'s tools never put SQL on the wire. The two halves aren't alternatives: a gateway can carry caller identity; it can never carry emission, because emission requires seeing the actual data operation. `README.md` § *Where the instrumentation sits* states this plainly. | Mitigated by D17 — the identity half is live (D10); the emission half is structural, not eliminated, and adopting it is down to the ecosystem (A12). |
| R07 | The demo's lineage consumer may not understand the mechanism the RFC now rests on. `ParentRunFacet` 1-2-0 (which D10 relies on) landed upstream 2026-07-08; the pinned Marquez image runs the older jar 0.51.1, and Marquez has cut no release since 0.50.0 (2024-10-24). Whether facet forwarding is ingested and displayed was unverified. | Created by D10. | Unknown before probing — checkable directly by emitting an event carrying `parent.run.facets` and reading it back. | Medium, and about demonstration rather than correctness — OpenLineage facets are additive and unknown fields are ignored, so events should still be accepted; the exposure is that the demo can't *show* forwarded identity in the UI. | Probed directly (`probes/verify_parent_facet_forwarding.py`) against Marquez 0.51.1. Linkage survives — `parent.run.runId`, job namespace/name, and the whole `root` block come through intact. Forwarding at the parent level is silently discarded — `parent.run.facets` and `parent.job.facets` are absent even from the raw stored event, not just the UI. Forwarding at the root level survives verbatim. Both events were accepted with HTTP 201 and no warning either way. Don't depend on parent-level forwarding — emit the parent run's own event carrying the identity facets, use `ParentRunFacet` for linkage only, and forward on `root` as well since that's what actually survives. | Closed — the question is answered, and the answer is folded into D10's design; see I22 for the Marquez bug this probe surfaced. |
| R08 | A reviewer in a regulated setting rejects fail-open as the wrong default. The design holds that a lineage failure must never break the tool call, so a query can execute with no record that it executed — in a control environment, that's precisely the state a control exists to prevent. | Created by D11. | Medium-high once `REQUIREMENTS.md` reaches its intended audience — this is the first question a control function asks of any observability side-channel. | High on credibility rather than correctness. An unstated fail-open default reads as an oversight; a stated one with the alternative offered reads as engineering judgement. | Made the posture configurable, not assumed. `LINEAGE_FAILURE_MODE` selects fail-open or fail-closed on the driver; an unrecognised value refuses to start rather than silently defaulting. The START-before-execution ordering is what makes fail-closed real rather than theatrical — the failure to record is caught before the data is reached. Two carve-outs: the failure path itself stays fail-open in both modes (a lineage error must never mask the underlying database error), and the drop is counted before the mode is consulted, so fail-closed can never become the reason a loss goes unrecorded. Verified end to end against `mcp-server-failclosed` in compose — same image, pointed at a collector that doesn't exist, with `FailureMode` set to `closed`. The tool call was refused, and the server log showed exactly one emission failure (START) and no execution. 5 unit tests plus 1 integration test cover both postures. | Closed — both postures implemented and verified; neither is presented as the correct default. |
| R09 | Identity may be deliberately absent from telemetry, and the correlation reference that replaces it may dangle. (a) Mature OTel deployments routinely strip identity-bearing attributes as a data-protection control — the same instinct R02 states as our own design principle, so a well-governed deployment may have actor identity absent by policy, not oversight. (b) Where identity is stripped, the remaining link is `trace_id`/`span_id`, but that's only resolvable if the trace was retained, and sampling is the norm. Our own `otel-collector-config.yaml` tail-samples at 10%, so roughly nine in ten lineage events carrying a trace reference would point at a trace Jaeger never stored. | R02, D01, D10; compounds R01. | High — (a) is standard practice in any regulated deployment and (b) is the default in almost all of them. | High, and it cuts at the fallback rather than the primary path — if `_meta` carries lineage parentage the primary path is unaffected, but the whole argument for trace correlation as a safety net assumes the trace is both identity-bearing and retained, which in a well-run organisational deployment it's frequently neither. | Don't treat trace correlation as an identity mechanism — it's a correlation mechanism, and only where the trace is retained. The lineage event carries a pseudonymous actor reference supplied by the caller in `_meta`, resolvable in a separate access-controlled system, which survives both stripping and sampling because it never depended on the span. | Open, partially mitigated. Leg (b) — durability — is addressed: a `lineage.dropped` span attribute plus a first-position `string_attribute` tail-sampling policy keep any trace recording lineage loss, unconditionally, while everything else stays at 10%; verified in Jaeger. Leg (a) — deliberate identity stripping — is untouched, and is exactly why identity has to travel in `_meta`, not the span. |
| R10 | The objectives get confused, and the workstream is read as duplicating something that already exists. Three distinct records are routinely conflated: platform audit (what the engine did), OpenLineage (what data an actor reached and why), and traces (the decisioning context). The predictable challenge: "Snowflake/Databricks/Fabric already audit data access — why do I need this?" | D14; compounds R06. | High — it's the first question a Chief Data (and AI) Officer (CDAO) asks, and the honest answer isn't obvious. | High on credibility. Answered badly it reads as either ignorance of the platforms or empire-building; answered well it's one of the strongest sections in the publication. | Verified against primary documentation, Snowflake only. `ACCESS_HISTORY` gives one row per statement, column-level access resolving through views — genuinely stronger than our own parse — but no SQL text, latency up to 180 minutes, retention 365 days. Reasoned but not verified: Iceberg REST catalogs vend scoped credentials, after which the engine reads storage directly — on that pattern, catalog audit records authorisation, not access; reasoned from the catalog design, not from vendor documentation. Not researched at all: Databricks/Unity Catalog, Microsoft Fabric/Purview, AWS (Redshift, Lake Formation, Glue, SageMaker). The claim that generalises: platform audit sees the connection, not the actor behind it, and never sees purpose — structural, true wherever the audit is platform-native. Everything about a named product's latency, retention, or capture detail is Snowflake-only until each platform gets the same treatment. Don't claim platform audit is insufficient — claim it's a different record: the agent is invisible (a pooled service identity means every agent and every human behind it arrives as, e.g., `SVC_MCP_PROD`); purpose is absent everywhere; it doesn't cross systems; latency and retention are set for reporting, not control. | Open — landed in `REQUIREMENTS.md` § 2a; still to land in the blog draft. |
| R11 | The trace-context proposal is read as an attempt to make OpenLineage a telemetry tool — the opposite of its intent. An OpenLineage maintainer has already stated, on [#4588](https://github.com/OpenLineage/OpenLineage/issues/4588), that "it's not a monitoring or observability tool, it has a different purpose." A facet carrying a trace ID is the most obvious possible trigger for that objection. | A08, D15, D12. | High — it's the default reading, and the word "trace" in a lineage spec invites it. | High, and it spreads — a close on scope grounds takes the credibility of the other two asks with it, since all three are filed on the same argument. | Lead with the boundary, not the mechanism — the two planes (data/ information observability and software/infrastructure observability) remain orthogonal, and the facet is a reference across a seam, not a bridge that merges them: one pointer, two identifiers, no metrics, no health semantics, omitted where the mapping doesn't hold. `ParentRunFacet` is the precedent — a pointer to another run that doesn't import the parent's concerns. State why the seam exists now: before agents, the two planes stayed separate because the actor was a scheduled job — durable identity, fixed purpose, knowable from the job definition. An agent creates the consumption event and the decisioning context in the same instant, as the same act, so the two records are now linked while remaining different kinds of fact. | Open — written into the RFC draft and into `REQUIREMENTS.md` § 10; still to land in the blog draft. |
| R12 | The gap is growing fastest exactly where the workstream doesn't reach. Agents read and now write unstructured data — documents into object stores, embeddings into vector indexes, generated content pushed back into systems of record. A structured/SQL-scoped answer addresses a shrinking share of how agents actually touch information. | Created by D16. | High and rising — a direction of travel, not an event. | Medium on the narrow claim, which stands on its own; high on positioning — the risk is being read as having solved the easy case and missed the important one. The obstacle underneath is real: dataset identity for unstructured retrieval is unsolved. What is "the dataset" when an agent retrieves three chunks from a vector index — the index, the source document, the chunk? OpenLineage naming answers none of these. | Name it as the known next problem in the publication, with the identity question stated as the reason it's hard rather than the reason it's skipped. Sequence it as its own workstream once the structured case is filed. | Open — to be written into `README.md`, `REQUIREMENTS.md`, and the blog. |
| R13 | The demo is 100% governed traffic, so it can't show the sampling contrast its own README claims, and an OTel-literate reviewer may reasonably call that unrealistic. Every driver call is `execute_sql`, so every span carries `lineage.run_id` and is unconditionally kept by the `always-keep-data-access` policy. In a real deployment, governed-data touches are a minority of traffic, and OTel's tail-sampling processor decides per trace, not per span — so one governed span pulls the entire trace through, ordinary spans included. | Live re-verification of I32; `otel-collector-config.yaml`'s `always-keep-data-access` policy. | Medium — requires a reviewer to notice the 100% ratio and reason about trace-level retention economics, but plausible from an OTel-literate audience. | Medium-high on credibility if raised publicly and unanswered ("your demo is too clean, real traffic doesn't look like this"); low on the underlying argument, which doesn't depend on the ratio. | Two tracks. State the trace-level retention semantics and its cost implication explicitly in `README.md`; build a proof that actually shows one governed span pulling a larger ordinary trace through (I34). | Closed. Both responses applied — `README.md` § *Why every trace is kept here* discloses the behaviour, and I34's mixed-trace proof was built and verified live against a real stack. |
